## B09｜B2(a)｜−78 steps

Section B question two part (a). Eight-bit two's complement reading: one zero one one zero zero one zero.
Step one: most significant bit M S B is one — so the number is negative.
Step two: invert all bits — zero one zero zero one one zero one.
Step three: add one — zero one zero zero one one one zero.
Place values: sixty-four plus eight plus four plus two equals seventy-eight.
So the decimal value is minus seventy-eight.
Do not ignore the sign bit and write plus seventy-eight; do not invert without adding one.

## B10｜B2(b)｜MSB as sign bit

Section B question two part (b).
In two's complement, the most significant bit M S B is the sign bit.
M S B equals one means negative.
M S B equals zero means non-negative — positive or zero.
The sign bit takes part in the numeric value and decides the sign.
Do not only say “it is important” without explaining plus or minus; do not call the M S B a parity bit.

## B11｜B2(c)｜Odd parity

Section B question two part (c). Data bits one zero one one zero zero one — odd parity.
Count the ones: four — an even count.
Odd parity needs the total number of ones, including the parity bit, to be odd.
So set the parity bit to one.
After that there are five ones — odd, as required.
Setting zero is even-parity thinking — wrong here.

## B12｜B3(a)｜Bitmap vs vector

Section B question three part (a). Compare how they are built.
Bitmap: a grid of pixels, or a raster of dots.
Vector graphics: defined by mathematical objects — lines, curves, shapes.
A good vector example: company logo, typeface, line art, or C A D drawing.
Reason: objects scale and edit independently; suit many output sizes.
This part does not test enlarge-and-jag; do not only talk about distortion.

## B13｜B3(b)｜Sampling vs quantization

Section B question three part (b). Two steps in image digitising.
Sampling: measure or capture image samples at set spacing in space or time; linked to resolution or sampling rate.
Quantization: map each sample’s continuous brightness or colour onto a finite set of discrete levels; linked to colour depth or bit depth.
Do not swap the two definitions.
